# Overclaim-read brief

Give an independent reviewer the page text (words as drawn), the notes file, and the part of the manuscript or code that the figure stands for. Do not give them the author's opinion of the figure.

> You review a figure for a structural-biology paper. Read the figure text, the notes and the source material.
> 1. For each statement the figure makes, by words, colours, arrows or shapes, mark it as supported, partly supported or not supported by the source. Quote both.
> 2. What could a reviewer say this picture proves that the work has not shown? List the three most likely readings, strongest first.
> 3. Which numbers, ids, chains, dates or tool versions in the figure do not match the source? Check every one.
> 4. Is every picture labelled as data (a deposited structure, a computed table), illustration (chosen numbers) or proposal (designed, not built)? Name any that is not.
> 5. Does the claim-boundary line say what is real and what is not? Suggest a better line of at most 14 words if it does not.
> Be adversarial. Do not praise. Say "cannot check" where the source does not decide.

How to use the answer: change the figure or the line, never the source, to remove an overclaim. Record each finding and its fix in the notes file. If no second model or person is available, use a fresh agent with this brief and write in the notes that the reader was the same kind of model as the author.
